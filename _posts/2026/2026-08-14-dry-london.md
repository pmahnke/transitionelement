It hasn't rained since 26 June 2026. That's 50 days without rain.

The current situation is historically unprecedented. The UK is experiencing one of its most severe droughts on record, and the South East of England is the hardest-hit region.

Here are the staggering statistics and impacts for London right now:

* **The Driest Month Ever Recorded**: July 2026 wasn't just the driest July on record; provisional Met Office statistics confirmed it was the driest month ever recorded for Southern England (dating back to 1836).
* **Near-Zero Rain**: You would normally expect around 45 mm of rain in July, Southern England averaged an astonishing 1.9 mm (just 3% of the normal amount). Greater London was one of 19 counties that recorded less than 1 mm of rain for the entire month.
* **August continues to be dry**: The dry spell has continued relentlessly into August. As of right now (mid-August), rainfall has only been about 5% of the seasonal average.
* **Thames Water Restrictions**: Because reservoir storage and river flows have plummeted, Thames Water instituted a strict Temporary Use Ban (hosepipe ban) across Greater London and the Thames Valley on July 23, 2026.
* **Local Impact in London**: The drought is incredibly visible in South West London. Local reports have highlighted that public spaces like Moormead Park, Richmond Park, and parks everywhere around us are completely scorched brown. 

Overall, instead of the normal ~80 mm (3 inches) of rain you would expect over the last seven weeks, London has received barely a few millimeters, officially placing the entire region into Drought status according to the Environment Agency.

We have also seen a lot of trees start to lose their leaves early and several trees around us have dropped major branches, or just fallen over (including on our road).

Global warming is real and we are not reacting and changing fast enough.
