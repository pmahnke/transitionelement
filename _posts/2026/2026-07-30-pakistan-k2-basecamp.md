### 7 to 30 July 2026

In July, I flew to Pakistan to join a group guided by [Spantik Expeditions Treks and Tours](https://www.spantikexpeditions.com/trekking/karakoram-range) to go to K2 Basecamp, the second highest mountain in the world (8,611 meters / 28,251 feet) and one of the most dangerous to climb. We were a group of eight trekkers, helped by a team of close to 20 others, guides, cooks, porters, muleteers who helped make it all possible. Not to mention two goats and several chickens that started the hike with us and mysteriously disappeared over the next 12 days! 

After flying to Islamabad, there was a 40 minute flight to Skardu where our group spent 2 nights before the Trek started. We did a small walk up to an old fort in town and then to a tiny organic village where we were treated to apricots and tea. The Indus River flows right beside the town. Was nice to hear the call to prayer several times a day from all the mosques in town. As our hotel was located up on the hillside we had a good view of the surrounding mountains.

#### Day 1

We drove for five or six hours by jeep along narrow cliff side roads next to the roaring river until we could go no further. We then had to cross a rickety bridge on foot (they were rebuilding a larger footbridge next to it but it was not open yet). Porters carried all of our stuff across and we set off in a new jeep for another hour until we reached our first campsite at Askoli - 3,000 meters (9,843 feet). A small village and the last one we would see until the end of the trip. Here we met the Pakistan crew who would come with us on our trek.

#### Day 2

A long first day of hiking, almost 20k, but relatively flat following along the river until we reached the Jhula campground. The mountains could already be seen all around.

#### Day 3

Another 20 kilometer day along the river to Paju campground where we got our first glimpse of the Baltoro Glacier that we would be hiking on.

#### Day 4

While this was a relatively short day mileage wise, it was uphill, all of the way, and we ended the day at Urdukas campsite 3,900 meters. The glacier was very rocky underfoot making for tough going on the trail. You had to watch your every step to make sure you were not going to twist an ankle. Along the way we had clear views in the distance of Trango tower, one of the greatest nearly vertical drops on earth and popular with climbers. The views from the campsite were amazing.

#### Day 5

Very rocky trail today, but it was fun to see "icebergs" along the trail. You could often see flowing rivers in the glacier. We spent the night at the Goro II camp reaching 4,200 meters. This was the first night actually sleeping on the glacier and it was definitely colder at night.

#### Day 6

A relatively easy day walking among icebergs until we finally reached Concordia camp 4,650 meters.  From here you get your first view of K2. We were blessed with the weather and had crystal clear views of K2 and the surrounding mountains. It is renowned as the "Throne Room of the Mountain Gods" because it offers unparalleled, panoramic views of four of the world's 14 "eight-thousander" peaks, including K2, Broad Peak, Gasherbrum I, and Gasherbrum II. 

We even had a beautiful sunset.

#### Day 7

Despite perfect weather so far, the weather was expected to turn the next day so the group decided to not move camp again to Broad Peak basecamp, but just hike and return the 20 kilometers to K2 basecamp - 5,150 meters (16,896 feet) and back. Our two strongest hikers walked there and back while four of us did it on horseback, and two took a rest day. How our guide magically found four horses is beyond me. But you did see horses all along the way, often used to help transport hikers who were not feeling well (usually stomach issues, as the ascent is gradual we did not hear of anyone having altitude sickness).

 The trip to K2 basecamp was along the Godwin-Austen glaciers and went past Broad Peek basecamp as well. When we got to K2 basecamp, we had tea and a snack at one of the climbing camps and spoke to a few of the climbers who were waiting for a weather window to start their ascent. We also went to see the Gilky memorial where there are plaques to those who lost their lives in pursuit of climbing K2 and the surrounding mountains - a far too large number.  In fact a week after we were there, 10 climbers died in an avalanche on Broad Peak, the mountain located right next to K2 - one of the worst climbing accidents on record.

#### Day 8

The weather did change and the clouds rolled in and we had rain for most of the day. The rest of the group had to make the decision if they were going to over the Gondogoro La pass or not. In the end they decided not to given the weather. We last learned a few other groups did go over, while several others made the decision not to as well. 

I was happy as it meant I had company for the walk down.

#### Days 9 to 11

We walked back out the way we came, in four days rather than the six up which meant 20k+ days each day. Of course it was easier as we were descending, but still long days of walking. While we had minimal rain, it was much cloudier so we had different views of the mountains. On day 11, once we got off the glacier, we were on the other side of the river for a while. We had a few interesting river crossings of the side tributaries flowing into the main river due to the rainfall higher up the mountains for the past few days. 

Thankfully, there was a bridge to get us back to the main path.

#### Day 12

We started very early as we had 10 kilometers to walk followed by the long drive back to Skardu. 

I have never been so happy to see a shower when we arrived back at Skardu!

The next day we flew back to Islamabad. Most of us had an extra day build in as flights from Skardu are often delayed or canceled due to weather. We took a city tour visiting the Faisal Mosque, which was closed inside unfortunately as they were getting it ready for Independence Day celebrations. We stopped at a scenic viewpoint where you could look down on the whole city, as well as the Pakistan monument. We also visited Taxila Museum and several of the ruins in the area.

Overall, it was a good but hard trip.
