After the disappointment of not seeing the eclipse in Spain, Angela was keen to see the Aurora (Norther lights). So off we went to Abisko, Sweden for a week.

We were meant to fly on the Sunday, but SAS canceled our flight for no reason.  This cost us hundreds on a room we cannot get the money back and a new rental car, because I couldn't modify the reservation I bought through SAS. And we had to go the next morning very early, which required a taxi, instead of a bus and tube to the airport. Annoying.

We flew to Kiruna, via Stockholm. Kiruna is a big mining city and we really only went to a grocery store and had a quick lunch before heading to Abisko, about 100 kilometers and one and a quarter hours away.


### Our cabin

We stayed, at the cute, [Abisko Mountain Lodge](https://abiskomountainlodge.se/) in one of their cabins.  Since we went for nearly a week, we wanted to be able to cook some meals and have a bit more space than a simple hotel room.

So, it isn't a lot of money, but we saved at least £450 by staying the cabin, more if you consider we also had lunch each day. For the extra comfort, I think it was worth it.

### Aurora tours

We went on two Aurora tours through the Abisko Mountain Lodge. They were excellent. The first one was a pretty horrible Aurora display, but the tour was still good and taught us a lot about how the Aurora and the various forecasts work. Because of that we went out on our own two more nights with much better success.

The tours are also nice because the take you some beautiful places and setup a fire to keep warm, give you hot lingonberry juice, and cookies.

### Norway

One clear day, we drove over to Narvik, Norway and, then drove the length of two fjords. It was very pretty and picturest. Narvik itself is pretty industrial. But it had nice shops and bakeries. It was a nice day trip. The trip iself was very intersting; over a pretty high pass with a lot of snow.

### Hiking

We didn't do a lot of hiking, but we did check out the Abisko canyon (Abiskojokk), walked the lake and the start of the Kungsleden trail. Again, minor rolling hills, but well marked and easy to find trails that fit your timetable.

### Weather

We arrived and it was around 9C (48F) and by the time we left, it was about 0C (32F). We had a bit of rain and a bit of snow, but it didn't stick where we were, just on the mountaintops. We brought nice warm down jackets, hats, gloves, scarfs. And underneath we wore a light fleece or jumper (sweater) and that seemed to work fine.

### Abikso

Abisko is tiny.  There are a few hotels and hostles. I think there is only the Abisko Mountain Lodge's restaurant and the one at the [Abisko Turiststation](https://www.svenskaturistforeningen.se/boende/stf-abisko-turiststation/). The Turistation has a really good shop for camping and clothing, so if you forget your gloves, head here.

There is one food/petro shop, [Godisfabriken Abisko](https://www.facebook.com/godisfabbrikenabisko).  Apparently, there was another food store that closed and this was a candy store, but now it has a very well stocked food section, you can find nearly anything for a week of meals here.

### Wine and liquor

In case you don't know, in all the Nordic countries, wine and liquor stores are run by the state and are physically separate from grocery stores. We knew this, so went to the [Systembolaget](https://maps.app.goo.gl/hq19krwnPQEXg6sc6) in Kiruna and bought a few bottles of wine. Oh, and the are closed on Sundays. However, you can by 3.5% beer in grocery stores.

### Driving

We found the cost of a rental car reasonable and very easy to drive as most of it is on the single road, the E10. The Swedes, and Norwegians drive on the right. Petrol/gas stations are like anywhere else in the USA/UK/Western Europe.

### Mobile/cellular service

There was nearly 5G everywhere. Sweden and Norway are free roaming destinations for our UK plans as well. I will say, I recommend downloading an offline Google map for Lapland anyway.

### Conclusion

I am glad we went. It wasn't the high season and our weather was a bit mixed, but we did have four our of six decent nights to see the Aurora.

I think you need to plan at least three nights to be sure you still see the lights. If you have any questions, please leave a comment.
